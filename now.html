From Full Stack to Multipotentiality: Embracing the Renaissance Within
By Gargi Yadav • April 2025

Have you ever felt overwhelmed by the question, 'What do you want to do with your life?'

Whenever someone asked me that, I’d think of the profession I mentioned last time and wonder why I ever wanted to do that in the first place. Then, I’d give a different answer.

At age 5, I was an artist who relished muralism and wore paint-splattered clothes like badges of honor. Then I saw my neighbor’s daughter in traditional folk-dance attire—and I wanted to be a dancer. (I still am, actually—college dance team and all.) In middle school, I performed poetry and debated. I learned the sitar, picked up the guitar, and dove into computing through Raspberry Pi.

By high school, chemistry had my heart, and I dreamt of being the next Jennifer Doudna. Then I discovered my first niche: computational chemistry.
Ring ring ring – there’s the sound of certainty.

Then came college. I became a triple major, convinced more than ever that this would be my path—a computational chemist who draws hyper-realistic portraits.

But somewhere along the way, I couldn’t imagine myself as just a lab-coat researcher, or just a corporate strategist in a suit, or just an artist sipping espresso in a studio in Europe. I started questioning my identity—wondering if I needed to choose just one thing.

I am my own niche. And that can change — and that’s okay too.
Fast forward to now, age 21.
I spend more time writing than I do spiraling. I’ve sat with all the hats I’ve worn, and the memories stitched inside each one. And I’ve come to see that I am a multipotentialite—a modern Renaissance soul. My ability to take on many roles, to thrive in both solitude and collaboration, isn't confusion. It's my core strength. Right now? I write. I design. I code. I cook. I build apps. I rest.
And most importantly: I give myself time to do what calls me.
My motto these days? I am my own niche. And that can change—and that’s okay too. I’m learning to admire the specialists around me, and to hold space for multidisciplinarians like me.In the end, embracing my multipotentiality has allowed me to live a richer, more authentic life. I am proud to be a modern Renaissance man.
— Gargi
